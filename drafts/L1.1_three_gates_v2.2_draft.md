# L1.1 Vulnerability — three gates (draft for v2.2)

## Checking the gates

**Checking the gates.** A span counts toward a vulnerability act only when all three gates hold.
- **Order.** Check V1, then V2, then V3. Stop at the first gate that fails, and record its exclusion.
- **Spans.** Check each candidate span on its own. A span that fails does not stop another span in the same turn.
- **Quotes.** A gate holds only when you can complete its test with quoted words.

In the Justification, tag the gates [Shortfall], [Owner] and [Stake], in that order.

---

## V1 · Real shortfall

V1 tests whether the words state a shortfall. A shortfall is something that counts against the person or group who has it. Who has it is V2.

A shortfall is one of four kinds:
- **Wrong or failed.** A claim, work, decision or conduct was wrong, or work was not done. A past failure to know or to act belongs here, for example "I didn't know the date last week".
- **Not known.** Something about the work is not known or understood now.
- **Cannot do.** Part or all of the work cannot be done, kept up with, or checked without help, now or ahead. A difficulty in health, family or private life, stated in words of difficulty, counts as a possible limit.
- **Falls short in skill.** Skill or performance falls short of what the team needs. A remark only about how the work looks is not enough.

**Named content.** The words name what the shortfall is about: a task, item, claim, capacity or need. Or they answer a teammate's question that names it, or accept a shortfall that a teammate has just named.

- **Test (the deletion test).** Delete the words that seem to state the shortfall. If the turn still says the same thing about the speaker and the work, those words were filler, and V1 fails.
  For example: "I don't know, whatever works." Deleting "I don't know" leaves the same meaning, so V1 fails (X14).

**Also not a shortfall.**
- **Taking on a task.** "… fountain supply line coordination, that's on me." [the lead, about an item that the lead took on earlier in the meeting]
- **"Should have" about the future.** "… I should have that pricing tomorrow."
- **A comprehension check.** "Does that make sense?"

### Exclusions under V1

- **X06 Giving way.**
  - Not a shortfall: the speaker drops or changes their own idea, option, estimate or proposal, or agrees or complies, and nobody said that it was wrong. Thinking aloud in joint problem solving, and a doubt about a detail that the team is checking together, belong here.
  - Counts: a teammate challenged the speaker's own claim, work, decision or conduct, and the speaker's words accept that it was wrong (Concession).
  - Does not qualify (X06): "Gotcha. Okay, I was thinking added drip pans somehow, but okay." [a member, after a teammate explains that drip pans are too big]
- **X09 New information.**
  - Not a shortfall: the speaker changes a plan, a date or a figure because new information arrived. The earlier claim was right when it was made.
  - Counts: the speaker says that their own earlier claim was wrong when they made it (Retraction).
  - Does not qualify (X09): "So I submitted it. … This morning I got an email saying that we gotta convert back to the original." [a member, about the member's own shop drawings]
- **X14 Formula.**
  - Not a shortfall: "I forgot" only introduces an item ("one more thing I forgot"); an apology or thanks concerns only the talk ("sorry, go ahead"); "I don't know" is filler; or "correct me if I'm wrong" opens a claim.
  - Counts: with the formula deleted, the turn still states a shortfall with named content. Or the evidence window names a failure in the speaker's work or conduct, not in the talk, that a bare "sorry", "my bad" or "I forgot" refers to.
  - Does not qualify (X14): "I don't know, we're all having a good time." [a member, in banter before the meeting]
- **X15 Empty self-remark.**
  - Not a shortfall: a joke about the speaker, mock self-blame, an exaggerated reaction to bad news from outside ("I give up"), a conditional apology inside a defense ("sorry if anyone was confused"), or a remark that softens the speaker's own question ("stupid question, but…"). The words name no task, item, work or skill.
  - Counts: the words name specific work, conduct or skill that the speaker assesses negatively or concedes. An apology with an "if" clause counts when it answers a shortfall that a teammate has just named and the speaker does not deny it.
  - Does not qualify (X15): "Just a stupid question here, but is that gonna hold that massive header…" [a member]
- **X16 Hedged answer.**
  - Not a shortfall: asked for a value, the speaker gives one with a hedge, such as "I think it's 40" or "probably 60".
  - Counts: the speaker also uses not-knowing words about the item (Not known).
  - Does not qualify (X16): "I'd say about five guys. 5, 6 more guys, that's it." [a member, asked how many more workers the member's crew will add]

## V2 · Own shortfall

V2 tests who owns the shortfall. It holds when the speaker's own words place the shortfall on the speaker or on their own group. A teammate can raise a shortfall, but only the speaker's words can own it.

- **The speaker names themselves.** The clause that states the shortfall has "I", "me" or "my". Owner: Self.
- **The speaker names their own group.** The clause has "we", "us" or "our" for a group that includes the speaker, such as the team in the meeting, the speaker's crew or the speaker's firm. Owner: Own group.
- **The speaker answers a direct question about their own task.** A short answer takes the subject of the question. "Did you send it?" followed by "Not yet." places the shortfall on the speaker. Owner: the one that the question names.
- **The speaker accepts a correction or challenge.** Words such as "you're right", "good catch" or "I had that wrong" take the subject of the correction or challenge. A bare "okay", "got it", "yeah" or "thanks" only acknowledges, and owns nothing.
- **The speaker asks for help or a check.** The speaker who asks owns the need. V2 holds when the request is for the speaker or their own group, not on behalf of another person.
- **The speaker shares a limit with others.** A first-person limit counts, even when the speaker says that it also applies to others.
- **The reason is the speaker's own circumstance.** The speaker's load, absence or oversight keeps the shortfall the speaker's own. "I forgot to send it" places the shortfall on the speaker. "[company] forgot to send it" never does.
- **The turn names an outside cause.** An outside cause is another person, a firm, a document, the weather, an event on site, a tool, or an answer that others still owe. V2 then needs words that also keep a share of the shortfall, for example "…but I should have checked".

- **Test.** Quote the owning words. If the turn names an outside cause, also quote the words that keep a share.
  For example: [Owner] "I forgot to send it" (Self).

### Exclusions under V2

- **X01 Outside cause.**
  - Fails: the turn puts the whole cause of the shortfall on an outside cause. This includes a gap that the speaker places in another person's unclear message, note or list entry, or in an answer that is still pending. Words that only state the shortfall, or that say that others share it, keep no share. When an outside cause explains why the speaker's own earlier date, figure or plan was wrong, words that only mark it as wrong keep no share either.
  - Counts: the speaker's words also keep a share.
  - Does not qualify (X01): "we didn't do the drop test because they didn't have it" [a member; "they" is an outside vendor]
- **X02 Other people's shortfall.**
  - Fails: the turn assesses another person's knowledge, work, conduct or experience, or says in general that nobody can know everything, and names no first-person shortfall.
  - Counts: the speaker's words also state a first-person shortfall.
  - Does not qualify (X02): "… A lot of people don't necessarily admit they don't know something." [the lead, after the team reads a company value]
- **X03 Reply to a teammate's vulnerability act.**
  - Fails: right after a teammate exposes a shortfall, the speaker only agrees, reassures or excuses the teammate, for example "I would have missed that too".
  - Counts: the speaker owns a share of the same shortfall in their own words (Unit of coding, Other speakers).
  - Does not qualify (X03): "That's okay. That's fine. … They knew what they needed to do." [the lead, right after a member says that the member did not know about added repair work]

## V3 · Interpersonal stake

V3 tests whether a teammate could hold the shortfall against the speaker. It holds when stake evidence shows what the team could expect of the speaker in this matter.

- **Where the evidence is.** In the speaker's own turn (Stake Evidence: Own words). Or in one of the two turns before it, or in a turn that the speaker's words point to (Stake Evidence: Context).
- **Which evidence to check.** Use the stake evidence for the candidate label (table below). If it fails, check the other labels that the words could take before you record an exclusion.
- **What is not stake evidence.** A question that only asks about status, such as "is it done?", even when it assumes that the work is done. A general remark on how the team should work.

| Label | Stake evidence |
|---|---|
| Concession: Welcomes correction; Concession: Accepts challenge | A teammate corrected or challenged the speaker's own claim of fact, completed work, decision or conduct. Always Context. |
| Owning-up: Retraction | The speaker gave the claim to the team in an earlier turn, as a fact, a date, a quantity or a decision, so that others could act on it. |
| Owning-up: Error | The work was due or committed: a date that has passed, "you were going to…", "I committed to…", a review of the team's committed items, or the speaker's words that a teammate must now wait because the speaker's part is not done. A fault word about the speaker's own task or conduct also counts. |
| Owning-up: Gap | The item is the speaker's own task, deliverable or area of work, or a teammate asked the speaker about it directly as the speaker's own item. |
| Owning-up: Limit | The limit affects the speaker's own assigned or committed work, or their work as a whole. |
| Seeking: Feedback; Seeking: Help | The work, plan, draft or judgment is the speaker's own. |
| Disclosure: Negative self-assessment | The judgment is about the speaker's own work or skill in this team's work. |
| Disclosure: Personal difficulty | The difficulty could change the team's view of the speaker, for example that they may be less available for a while. |

- **Test.** Complete the sentence "A teammate could hold it against the speaker that …" with a quote of the stake evidence. For a Personal difficulty, complete it with what the difficulty could change.
  For example: [Stake] u1451: "the email that the team agreed on last week" (Context).

### Exclusions under V3

- **X04 Progress report.**
  - Fails: the speaker says that work is not done, uses no fault word, and nothing shows that it was due.
  - Counts: words in the turn or the evidence window show that the work was due or committed (Error). Or a teammate asks the speaker directly for a judgment, not only for the status, and the speaker cannot give it because the work is not done (Gap).
  - Does not qualify (X04): "Not yet. I just got it this morning …" [a member, when the lead asks whether a price went to a subcontractor]
- **X05 Scheduling notice.**
  - Fails: the turn only tells the team when the speaker will be away, late or available, or when a meeting can take place, even with an apology, a personal or health reason, or "can't" about a date.
  - Counts: the speaker states a limit on their own work, not only on a date (Limit), or a personal difficulty in words of difficulty (Personal difficulty).
  - Does not qualify (X05): "I'll put in for a half day on the 19th. I got a doctor's appointment." [a member, in the review of staff time off]
- **X07 Another person's item.**
  - Fails: the speaker does not know about, or asks a clarifying question about, an item that another person or party owns or raised, or that the speaker reads aloud from a shared list.
  - Counts: a teammate assigned the item to the speaker, or asked the speaker about it directly as the speaker's own item (Gap).
  - Does not qualify (X07): "I know [name] kind of took that one over. … I have no idea what's going on with that." [a member reads the team's log aloud]
- **X08 Relayed-fact check.**
  - Fails: the speaker checks that they understood information from another party, for example "is that what they said?".
  - Counts: the speaker asks others to check the speaker's own work, plan or judgment (Feedback).
  - Does not qualify (X08): "they said they want it flush with the ceiling, right?" [a member, about the owner's team]
- **X10 Meeting mechanics.**
  - Fails: the speaker cannot find a file or a page during the meeting, has trouble with the call or the screen, or apologizes for such trouble.
  - Does not qualify (X10): "I'm back on, guys. Sorry, my phone was dead." [the lead rejoins the call]
- **X11 Routine request.**
  - Fails: the speaker asks for information, a file, or work from the person who normally provides or owes it, even with words such as "I need help".
  - Counts: the speaker asks for help with their own work beyond the helper's normal duty, or states a need for help with no named helper (Help).
  - Does not qualify (X11): "can you send me the Excel, the actual Excel version?" [a member, to the engineer who sent a list as a PDF]
- **X12 Lesson from elsewhere.**
  - Fails: the speaker tells a mistake from another job as a lesson or a warning.
  - Counts: the mistake is in this team's work, now or on an earlier project of this team; apply the gates as usual. Or the speaker ties it to their current work or skill; code the current shortfall.
  - Does not qualify (X12): "Trust me, I did it one time and I was the one counting it and somehow it was gone." [the lead, about hardware boxes that are still closed on this job]
- **X13 Wording repair.**
  - Fails: the speaker replaces a word, a name, a number or a date that the speaker said earlier in the same turn. Nobody could act on the first version.
  - Counts: the replaced claim comes from an earlier turn of the speaker (Retraction).
  - Does not qualify (X13): "Uh yeah, last Monday. Or no, sorry, it wasn't Monday. …" [a member, when the lead asks when the work started]

### Problems found and fixed in the draft

1. **Time.** The Gap section codes a past not-knowing as Error, and the Limit section codes a past inability as Error. The first draft kinds would have sorted these into Not known and Cannot do. Fix: Not known is now; Cannot do is now or ahead; a past failure to know or to act is Wrong or failed.
2. **Requests.** The owning rules look for "I" or "my" in the clause that states the shortfall. In Help and Feedback the shortfall is implied by the request, so "Can you look over this?" has no such clause. Fix: the speaker who asks owns the need; V2 fails only when the request is for another person.
3. **Bare acknowledgment.** The old rule 3 says that accepting words take the subject of a correction, but Welcomes correction says that a bare "thanks", "okay" or "got it" is only acknowledgment. Fix: V2 says that a bare acknowledgment owns nothing.
4. **Content tests in the stake list.** "Falls short of what the team needs" and "a remark only about how the work looks" (Negative self-assessment), and "in words of difficulty" (Personal difficulty), test whether there is a shortfall, not the stake. Fix: moved to the V1 kinds.
5. **X13 belongs at V3.** A same-turn slip is a wrong claim, and the deletion test does not remove it. It fails because nobody could act on it, which is the Retraction stake. Fix: X13 is under V3.
6. **Look-alikes with no gate.** Taking on a task, "should have" about the future, and a comprehension check had no gate. Fix: listed under V1 as not a shortfall.

### Open decisions (not applied)

- **X10 and X11 at V1.** Meeting mechanics are not about the work, and asking someone for what they owe shows no need beyond the normal. Both may fit V1 better. Moving them would leave V3 only about what the team could expect.
- **Question vs challenge.** This still decides between Concession: Accepts challenge and Owning-up: Error with Prompted (review item 7).
- **X-code numbers.** The codes no longer run in gate order (X06, X09 at V1; X13 at V3). Renumbering helps new coders but breaks the link to v2.1 data.

### Edits needed elsewhere

- **Evidence rule.** "V1 and V3 must hold in the speaker's own words" becomes: V2 must hold in the speaker's own words; V1's named content can come from a teammate's question that the speaker answers; V3 can hold in the evidence window.
- **Justification format.** Tags [Shortfall], [Owner], [Stake], in that order.
- **L1.1.3 Personal difficulty.** Match the definition to V1 (words of difficulty), not "recognizable social exposure".
- **"V1 to V3" references.** These stay, but the gates' meanings change. Add an old-to-new table to the version notes.
